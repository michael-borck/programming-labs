# Lab 12 — Ship Something True

> **Kills the belief that:** you need permission to ship.
> **Builds the habit of:** the full loop, owned.
> **Time:** a weekend, honestly — plan two or three sittings · **Needs:** everything you've
> got. No provided folder this time. That's the point.

## The brief

There isn't one. That's the final lesson: eleven labs ago a brief was something handed to you,
and now you know what most working builders know — **the brief was always going to have to come
from you.** Every product in this series started because someone decided something should
exist. Your turn.

Build a real thing, for a real person, and ship it. Not an exercise — the genuine article:
spec'd, checked, versioned, taught, verified, judged, polished, published, proven.

## Part 1 — Choose like a professional (first sitting, ~45 min)

1. **Write the itch list.** Ten candidates, fast, no filtering: the thing your club always
   needs, the calculator your workplace reinvents in spreadsheets, the tracker your family
   keeps almost keeping. Boring is fine — boring and *wanted* beats clever and orphaned.
2. **Apply the three constraints.** Your pick must be:
   - **For someone specific.** A name, not a demographic. You will send them a URL in Part 3,
     so choose someone who'll actually open it.
   - **Usable by a stranger in sixty seconds.** No manual, no you-standing-next-to-it.
   - **Honestly a weekend.** You've run `tiny-tweak`, you've kept a polish ledger, you did the
     Lab 10 retro — you *know* what your sizing instinct is worth. Halve the ambition; ship
     the half. (Cut scope is written down, not abandoned — that's what *Out of scope,
     deliberately* is for.)
3. **Spec it before anything else exists.** Fresh repo from the [`template/`](../template/),
   `SPEC.md` first — who it's for in one sentence, done-means a stranger could check, must-NOT
   doing its quiet work. Sharpen it with your agent (Lab 3's five-questions prompt), commit it,
   and only then let any building begin. That commit order — spec before product — is the
   series' signature. Sign it.

## Part 2 — Build with the whole kit (the long middle)

No new skills here. That's the design: this lab is the previous eleven, run consecutively, by
you, unprompted. The kit, as a checklist:

- [ ] **Teach the folder first** (6): `AGENTS.md` house rules, so every fresh session starts
      knowing you
- [ ] **Checks before features** (4): done-means becomes `checks/`; each new check fails once
      before its feature exists
- [ ] **Issues if it's more than one feature** (10): decompose, sequence, mark the cuts, one in
      flight at a time
- [ ] **Branch, PR, read the diff, merge** (5): the recorder is on; be fearless accordingly
- [ ] **Trust nothing fluent** (7): "done" gets a check run or an evidence request, every time
- [ ] **Log the deltas as they happen** (2, 11): every override, every surprise, one entry —
      your auditor is real now, you've met them, you've *been* them
- [ ] **The taste pass** (8, 9): polish ledger, before/after screenshots, the last 20% given
      its true weight

When you wobble — too big, stuck, bored at the 80% mark — that's not failure, that's the
curriculum arriving on schedule. Cut scope out loud, log it, keep shipping.

## Part 3 — Ship it (the moment)

1. **Publish** (1): GitHub Pages, live URL.
2. **Write the README for strangers**: what it is, who it's for, one screenshot, how to use it
   in one paragraph. First impressions are Lab 8 material — spend twenty real minutes here.
3. **Send the URL to your someone.** Not "check out what I made" — *"this is for you."*

Twelve labs ago, a URL working on your own phone felt like magic. This one is different: it's
on *someone else's* phone, because they wanted it there. That distance — from demo on your
laptop to product in someone's hand — is the exact distance this series claimed the job had
moved. You just walked all of it.

## Part 4 — Prove it (last sitting, ~45 min)

1. **The Replication Test, final boss** (3, 6): hand your `SPEC.md` — alone — to a fresh agent
   in an empty folder. Compare what comes out with what you shipped. Everything acceptable:
   you authored a program in English, and here's the executable proof. Every gap: your last,
   best homework — log it honestly. (Your spec won't fully pass. Nobody's does. Knowing
   *which* gaps matter is the expertise.)
2. **The critique, unsparing** (8): full `CRITIQUE.md` on your own shipped product, including
   the list you'd fix before charging money. Products you love get the honest version.
3. **The closing write-up**, in `DECISIONS.md`, one page, answering the question the whole
   series turns on:

   > **What did you do that the agent couldn't have? Point to the commits.**

   The spec that selected this product from the space of all products. The rejected sign-up
   screen. The check that caught the regression. The polish rounds. The cut you made to ship
   on time. The sentence you wrote for a nervous stranger. Point at each one. That pointing
   finger is your proof of work — and nothing autonomous produced it.

## Evidence checklist (the portfolio, complete)

- [ ] A live product a stranger can use, at a URL a specific someone received
- [ ] The full trail: spec-first commit, checks, issues, PRs, decisions, critique
- [ ] The Replication Test verdict, gaps logged
- [ ] The closing write-up, commits pointed at
- [ ] Twelve public repos on your GitHub profile — the evidence *is* the résumé. Pin the best
      ones.

## Going further (there is no further — there's just next)

- **Do it again.** The second product takes half the time. The fifth makes you dangerous.
- **Teach someone Lab 1.** Watching a beginner's URL land on their phone will show you how far
  you've come — and their questions will show you what you actually know.
- **The thirteenth lab** is whatever itch survived your itch list. It doesn't need us. It
  never did — that was the whole idea.
